# 01-star-atlas: notes

- Thesis: remoteness is darkness. The site is a celestial atlas plate at night: poles are stars sized by magnitude, the ranking is a star catalogue, and the quietest land shines brightest.
- What a visitor sees first: a map plate with a double neat line and a graduated edge. The imagery is taken down to a blue-black night. Top left is a catalogue entry: the unit name in Bodoni, the distance as a 60px figure ("3.43 km, from anything drivable"), the rank. The catalogue sits on the right.
- Signature: the stars. Authored SVG, and the disc steps down in size from pole 1 to pole 10. The italic Bayer-style number sits at a fixed offset beside the glyph and never scales. Selecting a pole blooms its star in 520 to 600 ms with an exponential ease-out, turning it into a gold glow with gold rays. The other stars dim to 62%. The same magnitude discs appear in the pole chips and in each catalogue row.
- Catalogue: rank, magnitude disc, name, then A and B as two aligned figure columns. The active scenario is in ink and the other is faint, rows are grouped by fives with a stronger rule, and the current unit is marked in italic. On desktop the title and column head stay fixed while the rows scroll.
- Fonts: Bodoni Moda (variable, opsz 6..96, weights 400..700, plus italic 400..600) for names, figures and captions. Jost 400/500/600 for data and controls, with tabular lining figures. Both come from Google Fonts, about 230 to 260 KB of woff2 with latin-ext.
- Colours: night #080d1b, starlight ink #e4e9f5, gold accent #f1cf7e. Bands run from cool blue through silver to warm gold (#4a70b4, #6c93d6, #93b3ea, #bccff2, #f3e0a6, #ffc94a) at 0.86 alpha, so the near bands read on the dark plate and the most remote bands are still the brightest. The imagery gets a CSS filter on the `.basemap` layer, and from zoom 12 a `map--near` class lifts it partly so the detail overlay can be judged against real terrain.
- Phone: the map plate fills the screen. The sheet is a night-catalogue drawer: the closed face shows the name in Bodoni and the magnitude in gold, and the card and catalogue scroll inside.
- Files changed: index.html (fonts, `color-scheme` meta set to dark, a shared SVG gradient defs block, `.plate` frame, key box, catalogue title and column head), css/app.css (rewritten), favicon.svg. JS changed: markers.js (star icons), card.js (entry head, filter control, chip magnitudes, no flags), ranking.js (paired A/B columns, magnitude disc, head), app.js (legend label span, zoom-12 class), i18n.js (new keys `magCaption`, `catRank`, `catName` in en and lt).
- Trade: single scheme. The night plate is the only scheme (`color-scheme: dark`, the same tokens under both prefers-color-scheme settings). The printed-paper light variant was built and then dropped after review, because it read as a grey, washed-out map and the stars lost their pull. Visitors who prefer light get the night plate anyway.
- Product changes implied: dark only (see Trade); country flags dropped from the card and the ranking. The headline sentence is kept only as visually hidden text; the name, figure and caption say it on screen.
- Risks: the dark plate hides terrain at country zoom (the Satellite/Map switch stays, and the night street map is an inverted OSM). Dark only is hard on bright daylight phone screens. In Lithuania at z7 the near bands now show, but only as scattered blue patches because little of the land is more than 1 km from a road; in the filtered imagery they can pass for lakes. Stars of neighbouring poles can still overlap each other when they sit close together (for example 8 and 10 in Lithuania). Bodoni hairlines thin out at small sizes, so Bodoni is kept at 13.5px and above.

Update dev/tests/card.test.mjs (the flag assertions, the `<p class="card__headline">` prefix, which now carries `vh`) and dev/tests/ranking.test.mjs if it asserts row markup or the "B x km" side text. Re-run the CI first-screen budget. `:has()` is used to tell the OSM tiles from the satellite tiles; older browsers fall back to the satellite filter on the street map.
- Detector: 2 "layout-transition" warnings for the phone sheet's height transition (kept from the incumbent; the height is measured by JS) and 1 "repeating-stripes-gradient" advisory for the plate's graduated edge (intentional: they are the scale ticks, not a surface texture). The hairline-border-plus-wide-shadow advisories were fixed by reducing the shadows to a tight 8px lift.
